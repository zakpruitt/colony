How the game works, how the code is put together, and the commands to run it. Things marked Built work today; Planned is designed but not yet in the game.

Where we are (16 November 2026)

In one line: the core loop is playable end to end on dev. 24 specs have landed (pull requests #2 to #25), the check passes, and nothing has gone to main. Zak approved spec 001; every spec after it was written and landed without him and is marked provisional until he has played it.

What is in the game

  • The loop: four heroes walk an endless labyrinth, fight, earn XP and gold, level up, rest, push deeper, wipe and fall back to the last stable depth.
  • Guardians: a boss holds the way down on every fifth level, with its name announced, a health bar at the top and a big death.
  • Spending gold: a shop with five camp upgrades, and an auto-buy switch.
  • Hero sheets: click a hero's frame (or press Tab) to see who they are, their health, XP, stats with upgrade bonuses, and what they are doing right now.
  • Idle: the game saves by itself and plays out the time it was closed (up to a limit), then shows what happened.
  • Feel: lit chambers, ambient life, damage numbers, bursts, coins, screen shake, a HUD, and 16 sound effects.
  • Cast: 4 heroes, 13 monsters across three depth bands, 4 guardians, 5 microskills and 5 side quests.

What to judge when you play

  • The sounds. Nobody has heard them; they were only checked on paper.
  • A guardian's death. It should be the best moment in the game. The first one is the Slime King at depth 5, and the party needs to be about level 9 to beat it.
  • Pacing. A simulated three hours with upgrades reached depth 17 at level 39 with one wipe. That may be too easy, or too slow to start.
  • The art. All of it is a first pass. Weakest: the ghoul, clay hound, fire imp and the Clay Colossus.
  • The zoom (Z) has never been seen on screen, only tested.

Known rough edges

  • Heroes vanish while the party is wiped, so the screen shows an empty chamber for a moment.
  • Melee heroes overlap a guardian's body in a fight.
  • The Bone Lord and Deep Maw (depths 15 and 20) were balanced by hand, with no test holding them.
  • Most pull requests after the first few were checked by CI and the lead only, not by a separate reviewer.
  • A save from testing may exist. For a fresh start, delete colony.save and colony.save.bak in %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Colony.

1. Run the game

From the colony folder, in any terminal:

godot --path .

To open the project in the Godot editor instead (press F5 there to play):

godot -e --path .

What you will see today: the party of four walking the labyrinth on its own, seen from the side like an ant farm, with monsters waiting in the chambers ahead. When the party walks into a chamber with monsters, the fight plays out: heroes and monsters wind up and strike, the mage and the cleric throw small bolts (amber to hurt, green to heal), the struck flash white and the dead collapse. Along the bottom, a frame per hero shows its portrait, level, health and XP; top left, the depth, what the party is doing and the target-depth controls; top right, the gold. The game keeps running while the window is minimised.

Fights, coins, level ups, wipes and the other beats of the loop have short 8-bit sound effects, quiet by default. Press M to mute or unmute them; the game remembers the choice (in a small settings file next to the save, not in the save).

If the game fails to start after pulling new work or switching branch, rebuild Godot's import cache and try again:

python tools/dev.py import

2. What the game is

Colony is a 2D pixel-art idle RPG you leave running while you do other things. A party of heroes lives in an endless labyrinth. They explore, fight, loot and rest on their own. You are the manager: you recruit, gear and spec the party, point it at a depth, and check in now and then.

Design pillars

  • Built to be left running. Leaving it alone is how it is played. Progress never goes backwards.
  • The skill is in building the party, not in fast reactions.
  • Flavour over power. Rare things give stories, not a must-have meta.
  • A rich, lively little world. Simple sprites, a lot of character in items and effects.

The full design is in docs/design/plan.md.

3. How a session plays Planned

  1. The party moves through the labyrinth on its own and fights what it meets.
  2. Kills give XP, gold and item drops.
  3. When resources run low the party rests, then carries on.
  4. You check in: spend XP on talents, buy and equip gear, change the party, set a target depth.
  5. You tab away again.

Built: spec 005 The loop runs in the simulation with nobody touching it, and Built: spec 008 the main game draws it (see "The world on screen" below). A party of four (knight, rogue, mage, cleric) starts in the middle of the start chamber and walks at 3 tiles a second, one behind the other, knight first. It has a target depth: above it, the party walks to the shaft and climbs down; below it, it climbs back up; at it, it walks from one end of the level to the other and back, farming. Every chamber except the start and the two chambers with a shaft holds 2 to 5 monsters (more in a den); deeper kinds are more likely once they appear. Walking into a chamber with monsters starts a fight. Each kill gives every living hero the monster's XP and the party some gold (double in a vault); enough XP raises a hero's level. After a fight, if anyone is dead or under 60% health, the party rests where it stands until everyone is full again (4% of health a second). A cleared chamber fills up again after 90 seconds. Each target depth that holds for the stable time is followed by the next one down (auto-push, which can be turned off), so the party keeps going deeper until it meets something it cannot beat.

Built: spec 020 Guardians. Every fifth level (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, ...) has a guardian in the chamber with the shaft down: one huge monster, alone, that the party must beat to go deeper. The Slime King holds depth 5, the Clay Colossus depth 10, the Bone Lord depth 15 and the Deep Maw depth 20 and every fifth level below. A guardian is a wall: four level-5 heroes almost never beat the Slime King, level-9 heroes almost always do (levels 10 and 15 against the Clay Colossus). The party throws itself at it, wipes, falls back to farm, grows and comes back. Beating one pays at least ten ordinary kills' worth of XP and gold, and its chamber then stays empty for 30 minutes so the party can pass freely. Guardians are never met in ordinary fights.

Built: spec 024 Guardian fights feel like boss fights. When the party meets a guardian, its name appears large in the upper middle of the screen (SLIME KING, with GUARDIAN OF DEPTH 5 under it) for two seconds, the screen shakes once and a low horn sounds. For the whole fight a wide red health bar with its name sits at the top centre, between the depth panel and the gold; it drains smoothly, flashes white on each hit and goes away when the fight ends. When a guardian dies the screen shakes hard, everything stands still for a fifth of a second, it bursts in three waves of its own colours, a fountain of at least 20 coins pours out, GUARDIAN SLAIN shows in gold and a rising fanfare plays. Losing to one just shows the usual WIPED.

Built: spec 025 Side quests and microskills. Not every chamber is a fight. On each visit, about one plain chamber in three holds a side quest instead of monsters: a locked chest, a stuck door, a hurt wanderer, a rune stone or a tripwire. Each needs a microskill, a small talent a hero picks up as they level: the rogue becomes a Lockpicker at level 2 and a Trapspotter at 6, the knight a Strongarm at 3, the cleric a Mender at 3 and the mage a Runereader at 4. If someone in the party has it, the party stops for 3 to 6 seconds (the HUD says Working) and gets the reward: gold worth one to three kills of that depth, or, from a rune stone, XP worth two kills for every living hero. Then the chamber is cleared and fills again after the usual 90 seconds. If nobody can, the party walks past and the quest waits for a later pass, once someone has learned the skill; a tripwire instead springs and every hero loses a fifth of their health (never down to 0, so it cannot wipe the party). Early on the party walks past a lot; a stronger party unlocks more. Nothing is drawn for them yet besides the HUD word: that is a later spec.

Death and the last stable depth

There is no permadeath. The game remembers the deepest area where the party survived for a set time (about ten minutes to start). If the party wipes, it revives there and keeps farming. A wipe costs efficiency, never progress.

Built: spec 005 A depth becomes the last stable depth after ten minutes on it without a wipe. After a wipe the party stands up again five seconds later, at full health, where the shaft lands on its last stable depth (or in the start chamber), and that becomes its target. XP, gold and levels are never lost. A fight still going after 90 seconds counts as a wipe: the party is overwhelmed. If you set a target depth while the party is down, it keeps your choice when it stands up. To watch an hour of this as numbers, run the sim command in the table below.

Party

One to four heroes from a small classic set of classes: tank, healer, melee damage, ranged damage. Balance matters: a party with no healer should struggle at depth. Loot is randomised from standard base types.

The labyrinth

Endless and generated as the party explores, from a seed, so the same seed always gives the same labyrinth. Difficulty rises with depth.

Built: spec 003 The simulation generates the levels. Each depth is a band of earth 120 tiles wide and 16 tall with four to six chambers on one flat floor, joined by low tunnels. Each level has one shaft down, on the right on odd depths and on the left on even ones, so the party crosses most of a level to go deeper. Chambers are plain, a den or (at most one per level) a vault; the first chamber of depth 1 is the start. A level is only made the first time something asks for it.

Built: spec 006 The labyrinth is drawn as a cross-section, like an ant farm: earth with the chambers, tunnels and shafts cut out of it, a ladder down every shaft, and sky and grass above depth 1. Floors have a bright top edge, ceilings and walls a ragged outline. The earth changes every five levels (brown loam, then clay, then grey stone, then deep teal rock, then round again) so going deeper looks like going somewhere. Only the levels next to the action are kept in memory. A camera follows the action smoothly and can shake. A demo shows it on its own (see the table below).

The world on screen Built: spec 008

The main game joins the simulation, the labyrinth and the sprites. The camera follows the party, looking a little ahead of it, and glides down shafts with it. Each hero walks smoothly between the simulation's steps; when the heroes line up for a fight, regroup after one or step onto a ladder, they run to their places rather than jumping. Monsters stand idle in every chamber that holds them on the levels near the party, facing the party's side, and reappear when a chamber refills. In a fight every attack, hit, death and bolt comes from the simulation, so what you see is what happened. After a wipe the heroes stay down until they stand up again where they revive. If the game falls behind (a long freeze, say), it catches up at once to the simulation's true state instead of replaying old animations.

Built: spec 012 The labyrinth is lit and alive. Underground is dark; each chamber has torches on its back wall that throw warm, flickering pools of light in a few hard-edged rings (pixel art, not a smooth blur), and the tunnels between are darker. Light stops at the walls: the lit shape is the room's shape, and the solid earth stays a dim, even mass you can always make out. The party carries a light of its own, so the heroes always show their true colours, even in a dark tunnel. The earth holds things to notice: stones, roots near the surface, bones, fossils and ore that glints now and then. Each kind of chamber looks like itself: the start has a campfire and bedrolls, a den has webs, a hanging spider, bones and a nest, a vault has a chest, coins and a gleam of gold, and plain chambers have a few stalactites, mushrooms, a broken crate or a puddle. Something is always moving: flames, rising embers, dust drifting in the light, drips that splash on the floor, a beetle crossing a floor or a moth circling a torch. The mood follows the earth: warm in the loam and clay, cold and darker in the stone, an eerie teal in the deep, where torches burn teal and glowing mushrooms and crystals grow. Nothing here touches the rules, and the same seed always looks the same. It is not in the main game yet: a demo tours it (see the table below).

Combat effects Built: spec 010

Every hit is felt. A number pops out of whoever was hit and arcs up: white on monsters, red on heroes, and a big gold number with a ! for a critical hit. Sparks fly away from the attacker and fall. A crit also shakes the screen a little and holds every character still for a split second; a hero dying shakes it harder. Heals show a green +N with sparkles. A monster that dies bursts into bits of its own colours with a pale puff, its coins spill out, bounce on the floor, rest a moment and fly to the purse at the top right (one coin per 5 gold, up to 12), and blue XP motes fly to each hero who earned them. A level up throws gold sparks and shows LEVEL UP. Bolts leave a short trail and flash where they land. Resting heroes give off a small z now and then; a wipe shows WIPED until the party stands up again in a column of light; a new last stable depth shows DEPTH n STABLE. Effects never fade: they shrink, blink or fly away. After a long freeze old events start no effects (their gold is counted at once), and there are never more than 40 numbers and 300 particles on screen.

The HUD Built: spec 011

Everything you need to manage the party, at the edges of the screen so the action stays clear. Each piece sits on a small dark panel so it reads over the sky and the darkest rock alike.

  • Party frames (bottom), in marching order: each hero's portrait, class, level, a health bar (green above half, yellow down to a quarter, red below) that drains smoothly, and an XP bar toward the next level. A hit flashes the frame; a dead hero's frame goes dark with a skull until it stands up again; a level-up makes the level number pulse and the XP bar start again from empty.
  • Gold (top right), with commas. It counts up as coins arrive; if it is still behind 3 seconds after the last coin, it catches up at once.
  • Depth (top left): the current depth in big numbers (it lights up when it changes), the last stable depth, the target depth, and one word for what the party is doing (Travelling, Fighting, Resting, Climbing, Reviving, Working).
  • Controls: - and + (or the - and = keys) lower and raise the target depth, never below 1. Auto (or the A key) turns auto-push on and off; it is green when on. If auto-push raises the target, the HUD shows it.
  • Z zooms the labyrinth in to twice the size, centred on the party, and back out again (spec 018). The change eases over a quarter of a second; the HUD stays the same size.
  • F3 shows or hides a developer readout of the simulation's tick and seconds, in the bottom-right corner.

The upgrade shop Built: spec 017

The Shop button sits left of the gold (or press U). When the party can afford at least one camp upgrade, a small amber tag on the button pulses and shows how many. The shop opens as a panel under the gold, between the top of the screen and the party frames, and the game keeps running behind it. Each row shows an upgrade's name, what it does, its rank (Rank 3/20), the bonus it gives now (+15%) and a button with the next rank's price: yellow when you can pay, dim red (and doing nothing) when you cannot, and MAX at the top rank. Click the price, or press the row's number (1 to 5), to buy: the row flashes green and the gold drops at once. U or Esc closes it; the depth keys, Z and F3 still work while it is open. Opening the shop closes the "While you were away" panel if it is still up.

Auto-buy Built: spec 022

At the bottom of the shop is an Auto-buy toggle (or press B while the shop is open). It is off in a new game and reads Auto-buy off; when on it turns green and reads Auto-buy on. While it is on, once every second of game time the camp buys the cheapest upgrade the party can afford (the one higher in the list on a tie), exactly as if you had clicked it: the row flashes and the gold drops. It never spends gold the party does not have, and it stops by itself once every upgrade is at its top rank. The switch is saved, and it keeps buying while the game is closed: the "While you were away" panel then says how many upgrades were bought.

The hero sheet Built: spec 026

Click a hero's frame (or press Tab) and a panel opens under the depth panel at the top left with everything about that hero; the hero's frame gets a yellow edge so you can see whose sheet it is. Click another frame, or press Tab again, to move to the next hero; click the same frame, the small x, or press Esc to close it. The sheet and the shop never show together: opening one closes the other. The game keeps running behind it, and every other key still works.

  • Who: the hero's portrait at twice the size (a skull while they are down), the class, the role in one word (Tank, Striker, Caster, Healer) and the level.
  • Doing now: one coloured line: Walking, Fighting Goblin (the monster they are attacking), Healing Knight, Resting, Climbing, Down in red, or Recovering while the party stands back up.
  • Where they stand: health as numbers and a bar (during a fight, the health they have in that fight), and XP toward the next level (30 / 50 XP, 20 to level 2).
  • What they can do: Health, Damage, Armor, Attack speed (attacks a second), Crit chance, Range (Melee or tiles) and, for the Cleric, Healing, with the camp upgrades already counted in. A stat an upgrade raises shows the bonus beside it in green (Damage 14 (+15%)).

Everything on it is read from the game every frame, so it is right after a level up, a purchase or loading a save. Later specs will add per-hero upgrades and skills to it.

4. How the code works

The code is in two halves that are kept strictly apart.

HalfFolderJob
Simulation sim/ Every rule of the game: movement, combat, loot, rest, depth. Plain code with no graphics, no clock and no global randomness, so it can run with no window at all.
Game game/ Shows the simulation (sprites, effects, UI) and passes the player's choices to it. Decides no rules.

This split is what makes the rest possible:

Time Built

The simulation moves in fixed ticks, ten per second of game time, and never asks what time it is. A driver measures real elapsed time and steps the simulation that many ticks. Because ticks follow the clock and not the frame rate, the game keeps exact time when unfocused or minimised, and time missed while the game was closed is simply more ticks to work through.

Randomness Built

Every dice roll comes from a seeded stream (SimRng). The same seed gives the same run, which makes bugs repeatable and balance testable. Each system takes its own stream, so adding a loot roll never changes what combat rolls. Watching the game never changes it: a game on screen and the same seed run headless (the sim command) play out exactly alike, monster for monster (spec 023).

Content as data Built

Classes, talents, items and monsters are data files under data/, not code. Adding a monster means adding a file. Every data file is checked automatically. So far there are four hero classes (data/heroes/: knight, rogue, mage, cleric) and thirteen monsters plus four guardians (data/monsters/), five microskills (data/microskills/) and five side quests (data/sidequests/). Each band of five levels has its own inhabitants (Built: spec 013): slime and cave bat from depth 1, skeleton from 2, spider from 3, goblin from 4 and ogre from 5 in the loam; mud golem from 6, fire imp from 7 and clay hound from 8 in the clay; ghoul from 11, crystal beetle from 12 and wraith from 13 in the stone; and the deep horror from 16. Deeper kinds are met more often once they appear, so each band is mostly its own monsters, and they are worth more XP and gold. A party of four level-12 heroes usually, but not always, beats three stone-band monsters; a level-6 party almost never does. Talents and items come later.

Camp upgrades Built: spec 016

Gold buys permanent upgrades for the whole party, rank by rank, each rank dearer than the last (the price of the next rank is its first price times a growth factor for every rank already bought). There are five, as data files in data/upgrades/: Sharp Blades (+5% damage and healing per rank, up to 20), Stout Armor (+5% maximum health, up to 20; buying it also heals by the health gained), Field Medicine (+10% healing speed while resting, up to 10), Quick March (+4% travel and climb speed, up to 10) and Fat Purse (+5% gold from kills, up to 20). Ranks add up: rank 3 of Sharp Blades is +15%. Rest, travel and gold bonuses apply at once; damage and health by the next fight. Upgrades are saved. You buy them in the upgrade shop on the HUD (spec 017). In a one-hour headless run, a party that keeps buying the cheapest upgrade it can afford holds a few depths deeper than one that buys nothing.

Fights Built

A fight happens on a chamber floor, as a line. Heroes come in from one side in formation (knight in front, then rogue, mage, cleric, a tile apart); monsters spread over the far half. Every tick, each fighter picks the nearest enemy (the cleric instead picks whoever is most hurt, if anyone is below 80% health), walks into range and attacks on a rhythm: a short wind-up, the strike, then a cooldown. Fighters on one side keep a little distance from each other (knight nearest the enemy, then rogue, mage, cleric), and whoever is held back behind an ally strikes over that ally's shoulder instead of waiting for a turn at the front. Melee strikes land at once; spells, spit and heals fly as projectiles. Damage rolls 85% to 115%, crits double it, armour takes some off, and every hit does at least 1. Monsters get 12% tougher for each level deeper than where they first appear, and heroes grow per level. The fight ends when one side is all dead (or, as a safety net, after five minutes of game time, which counts as a loss). Everything that happens comes out as a list of events for the screen to animate; nothing is drawn yet. The numbers are checked by tests that play hundreds of fights: a fresh party always beats three slimes, needs its cleric against a pack of skeletons, and loses to six ogres.

Art Built

Every sprite is typed, not painted: a small text file (.px) where each character is one pixel, using only the 32 colours of the master palette (assets/style/palette.txt). A tool bakes each text sprite into an ordinary image the game loads, and a lint refuses any art that breaks the rules: a colour outside the palette, the wrong size, a gap in the dark outline. Characters come in parts (body, head, arm, weapon) so the game can move them. The rules are in docs/style-bible.md, a first pass for Zak to change. To see every sprite, run the gallery (below).

Built: spec 007 The cast: four heroes (knight, rogue, mage, cleric) and five monsters (slime, cave bat, skeleton, spider, ogre), joined by spec 013's eight (goblin, mud golem, fire imp, clay hound, ghoul, crystal beetle, wraith, deep horror) and spec 020's four guardians (slime king, clay colossus, bone lord, deep maw, drawn 64x64, twice a hero's height), coloured for their band: warm in the clay, cold in the stone, teal in the deep. Each part of a character turns around its own joint (the neck, the shoulder, the hand), and the game moves the parts in code rather than flipping through drawn frames: characters breathe while they stand, bob and lean as they walk, draw back before they strike, flash white and flinch when hit, and collapse when they die. A demo shows the whole cast doing all of it (below); the characters are not in the labyrinth yet.

Text Built: spec 009

All text in the game is drawn in Colony's own small pixel font, typed the same way as the sprites (assets/fonts/pixel.px, one glyph per part). Capitals are 5 pixels tall, letters have their own widths (an i is narrower than an m), and every label uses it without being told to. Coloured text gets a one-pixel dark shadow so it reads over any background. To see every character, run the font specimen (below).

Saving

Built: spec 014 A save keeps the party's progress, not the exact moment: the seed, the time played, the gold, each hero's class, level and XP, the last stable depth, the deepest depth reached, the target depth, whether auto-push is on, and the rank of each camp upgrade (older saves load with no upgrades). Loading stands the party up fresh at its last stable depth, exactly as it does after a wipe, with everything it has earned; the chambers there are full of monsters again. A damaged or unknown save is refused rather than guessed at. The file is written whole or not at all (to a temporary file, then renamed into place), and the save before it is kept as a backup that is read if the save itself is missing or damaged. The save lives at user://colony.save.

Built The game loads the save when it starts, saves every 30 seconds, and saves when it closes. Tests, screenshots and headless runs never touch the real save. To start over, delete the save file (on Windows: %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Colony\colony.save and colony.save.bak).

Progress while you were away

Built: spec 019 Each save remembers the real time it was written. When the game starts with a save, it works out how long it was closed (at most 8 hours; under a minute counts as nothing, and a clock that went backwards counts as nothing) and plays that time through the same simulation as fast as it can, with the same rules as watching. Meanwhile the screen shows Catching up... and a bar that fills; eight hours takes a few seconds. Then the game starts as usual, with a panel in the middle of the screen saying how long the party was away, the gold it earned, the levels each hero gained, the depth before and after, monsters slain and wipes (and, with auto-buy on, the upgrades bought). A click or any key closes it, or it goes by itself after 12 seconds. No effects or flying coins are replayed for the time away. If you quit while it is catching up, nothing is lost: the save is untouched until the catch-up ends.

7. How work gets done

Colony is built spec-first. A spec says what a piece of the game must do and how we will know it does.

  1. Draft. Zak and the lead Claude session write the spec together.
  2. Approve. Zak approves it. Nothing is built from an unapproved spec.
  3. Build. An implementer agent builds exactly that spec in its own copy of the repo and opens a pull request.
  4. Review. CI runs the check, and a separate reviewer agent compares the spec with the code.
  5. Land. The lead merges it and marks the spec landed.
  6. Playtest. At the end of each milestone Zak plays the game and signs off.

The board and the full process are in specs/README.md.
