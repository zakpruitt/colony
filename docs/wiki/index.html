Where we are (17 November 2026): a demo to check in on

In one line: dev is a playable demo. Every spec written so far has landed (48 of them, pull requests #2 to #49), nothing is in progress, the full check passes (478 tests), and nothing has gone to main. Zak approved spec 001; every spec after it is marked provisional until he has played it. The direction for the world is in docs/design/world.md.

Play it

From the project folder, on the dev branch: git pull, then python tools/dev.py import once (new scripts need it), then godot --path .. For a fresh start, first delete colony.save, colony.save.bak and colony.settings in %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Colony; an old save loads too, and its heroes are brought up to the level their XP now earns.

Ten minutes with it

  1. Watch the first two minutes without touching anything. The top left says what the party is doing and counts down to when it goes deeper; the goals under it say what is coming. It should reach depth 2 in about 90 seconds.
  2. Spend the first gold. Click the Buy an upgrade goal when it appears, or press U. Heroes earn talent points from level 2: click the Spend the Knight's talent point goal, or the hero's frame and its Talents tab, and click a glowing node.
  3. Watch a side quest. A glint in the dark is one waiting. The rogue learns Lockpicker at level 2; before that the party walks past chests and the game says what was needed.
  4. Open the tally (T) after a few fights: who deals the damage, who takes the hits, who heals.
  5. Steer. Click a rung on the ladder at the right. Blue numbers are safe, orange within reach, red dangerous. Send the party somewhere red and watch it lose: the party lies where it fell, a note says who beat it and what would help, and it stands up again at its safe depth.
  6. Meet the Slime King on depth 5, about nine minutes in (sooner with F for 4x). It will probably win the first time. Try O and the Hold order, buy upgrades, then send the party back in.
  7. Close the game for a few minutes and open it again for the "while you were away" report.

To see every moment of the loop in ninety seconds, labelled, including the rare ones: godot --path . res://game/dev/showcase.tscn.

New since the first playtest

  • Side quests and microskills. About three plain chambers in ten hold a locked chest, a stuck door, a hurt wanderer, a rune stone or a tripwire instead of monsters. A hero with the right microskill (Lockpicker, Strongarm, Mender, Runereader, Trapspotter, learned by levelling) steps forward and does it; without one the party walks past and the game says what was needed.
  • The hero sheet. Click a hero's frame: stats, a Talents tab with the hero's whole talent tree (take a node, choose a path) and its paragon points to put into Power, Toughness or Quickness, and a Skills tab for their microskills.
  • Reading the party. A status line that says what and why, a countdown to the next stable depth, a journal (J), a depth ladder you can click to steer, and a "while you were away" report that tells the whole story.
  • Party orders (O): how hurt the party gets before it rests, and whether it fights guardians or holds until told.
  • Help in the game (H), tooltips on every control, and a game speed of 1x, 2x or 4x (F).
  • The party tally (T): a damage meter. Who dealt the damage, who healed and who took the hits, for the last fight or the whole session, as bars side by side. Click a hero's line to see its damage and healing by ability.
  • A livelier start. Depth 1 is stable in a minute, not ten (rising to ten minutes by depth 10), chambers near the surface refill in seconds, and early levels cost less. A new party reaches depth 2 in about 90 seconds and meets the Slime King after about nine minutes. These are the balance numbers most worth Zak's judgement.
  • Goals (G): the next few things within reach, with progress. Click one to go where you would act on it. The countdown now reads Deeper in 0:56, the moment the party will really move on.
  • After a defeat a note says who beat the party and why, and offers what would help: train a hero (with its paragon points), buy an upgrade, or hold at the guardian.
  • On screen: the party lies where it fell, the camera travels to where it revives and the heroes stand up again; melee heroes stand a sword's reach from a guardian; side quests glint in the dark.
  • A ladder that warns. Each rung's number is coloured safe, within reach or dangerous for this party; the target's rung has a yellow frame and the party's a white one; the mouse wheel scrolls it.

What is in the game

  • The loop: four heroes walk an endless labyrinth, fight, earn XP and gold, level up, rest, push deeper, wipe and fall back to the last stable depth.
  • Guardians: a boss holds the way down on every fifth level, with its name announced, a health bar at the top and a big death.
  • Spending gold: a shop with five camp upgrades, and an auto-buy switch.
  • Resources (spec 052): each area gives its own (glowcaps, clay, old bones, embers), and the later ranks of the camp upgrades cost one, so what you want to build decides where the party goes (see "Resources" below).
  • Hero sheets: click a hero's frame (or press Tab) to see who they are, their health, XP and what they are doing right now, with three tabs below: their stats, their talent and paragon points, and their microskills.
  • Talent trees and paragon: every class has a tree of eleven abilities, a trunk of three and two paths of four; heroes earn a talent point every second level and paragon points after level 14 (see "Talent trees and paragon" below).
  • Idle: the game saves by itself and plays out the time it was closed (up to a limit), then shows what happened.
  • Reading the party: the HUD says what the party is doing and why, counts down to the next stable depth, and keeps a journal (J) of what has happened; an arrow over the party points the way it is heading.
  • Game speed: watch at 1x, 2x or 4x with the small button beside Shop (or F); the button turns green above 1x. It is the same game, only sooner, and every session starts at 1x.
  • Help in the game (spec 030): the ? button or H opens a panel with the idea in five lines and every key; resting the mouse on any part of the HUD explains it in one sentence. A new game opens the help once by itself.
  • The party tally (spec 045): T, or the small button with three bars left of the hero frames, opens a damage meter on the left. Three tabs (Q E move between them) show each hero's damage dealt to monsters, healing done (only the health actually restored: healing past full health is never counted) or damage taken (traps included; damage dealt and taken count a killing blow whole, overkill included, as the tooltips say), as a bar in the hero's colour scaled to the largest, with the number and the hero's share. A switch picks Last fight (the fight under way while fighting; the line above the bars says what it was, where and how long: Last fight: 3 ogres, depth 7, 14 s) or Session (since the game was opened or loaded, with each hero's side quests under the bars). It counts only what happens while you watch: the time away is the away report's; a game opened in the middle of a fight shows that fight as the one under way. It is never saved, and it changes nothing about the game.
  • The depth ladder (spec 033): a column of depths down the right edge shows the whole descent at once: where the party is, the safe depth, how deep it has been, where it is heading and which guardians are beaten. Click a depth to send the party there; L hides it. Each depth's number says how it looks for your party (spec 043): blue is safe, orange is within reach, red is dangerous. The mouse wheel scrolls it. The target's number has a yellow frame and the party's a white one (spec 047).
  • Goals (spec 037): a small panel under the depth says what to look forward to next, each goal with a bar or a price; a hero's frame shows a small orange tag when there is something to do on their sheet. Click a goal to go where it is done: the shop, a hero's talents or skills, the orders, or the depth ladder (spec 042). G hides the goals.
  • The area map (spec 053): W, or a click on the area's name at the top left, opens a list of every area: its monsters (point at one for its name), its guardian with a green tick once beaten, the level of its first stage, and how far the party has got there (Safe 3, furthest 5). The area the party is in says Here; on the road the destination says On the way, 7 s. A closed area is dimmed and says which guardian opens it (Beat the Slime King in Mossy Hollow). Click an open area to choose it: a stage shows under the list, starting at that area's safe stage, with - and + from 1 to one past the furthest, coloured as the ladder colours depths (blue safe, orange within reach, red dangerous, by that area's levels). Go sends the party there (ten seconds on the road), or for the area it is in sets the target, as a click on the ladder does; a dangerous choice is warned of in the journal. Each row also shows the area's resource: its icon and how many the party holds (12 glowcaps), or for a closed area what opening it gives (Gives clay) (spec 052). A guardian stays ticked after a reload. When a guardian opens a new area, the journal says so, a goal Visit The Claypits appears (click it for the map) and the area's name at the top left carries a small orange mark until you open the map.
  • Party orders (spec 035): the Orders button (or O) opens a small panel with two standing orders the party follows by itself. Pace says how hurt it may get before it rests after a fight: Cautious (below 80% health), Steady (below 60%, the usual) or Bold (only below 40%). Guardians says whether it fights a guardian: Challenge (the usual) or Hold, where it turns back at the guardian's door and keeps hunting on that level until you choose Challenge; the status line then reads Holding at the Slime King. The current choice is lit green and explained in one line underneath.
  • Feel: lit chambers, ambient life, damage numbers, bursts, coins, screen shake, a HUD, and 16 sound effects.
  • Cast: 4 heroes, 13 monsters across three depth bands, 4 guardians, 5 microskills and 5 side quests.

What to judge when you play

  • Is it easy to follow? Could you say, at any moment, what the party is doing, why, and what happens next? That was the aim of this round.
  • Is there too much on screen? The top holds the depth panel, the boss bar, and Orders, speed, help, Shop and gold; goals sit under the depth panel, the ladder runs down the right, and the tally's button sits left of the frames. G and L hide the goals and the ladder.
  • The opening's pace. Depth 2 in 90 seconds, level 2 in under a minute, the first guardian at nine minutes. Too fast, too slow, about right? These are the only balance numbers changed this round.
  • The first guardian. Without upgrades, training or Hold, a new party loses to the Slime King about five times over twelve minutes before it wins. Is that a wall worth having, now that the defeat note says what to do about it?
  • The wipe. Does it read as a defeat and a return? The camera's travel crosses dark rock for a moment.
  • The ladder's colours. Orange and red are close at this size. Is the target easy to find?
  • Side quests. Do they feel like the party doing something different, and is "Needs Lockpicker" enough to make you want a different party? Their props are plain placeholders.
  • The sounds. Still nobody has heard them; they were only checked on paper.
  • 4x. Does a fight at four times speed still look like the same game?

Decisions waiting for Zak

  • Pace barely matters. Cautious rests four times as often as Bold but wipes almost as much (33 against 34 over three hours of testing). Should pace change more than when the party rests?
  • Fallen heroes stay on the field. A hero who dies in an ordinary fight now lies there until the fight ends, where before it vanished. This went beyond its spec and was left in.
  • Heroes stand further from a guardian. About 10 pixels of air, so that lunges and swings never draw over each other; before it was 2.
  • Cheaper levels reach the deep game. Levels cost about 12% less at level 10 and 6% less at level 20, which the first level-up inside 90 seconds needed. A three-hour run now ends at depth 18 to 21, where it ended at 17 to 18.
  • One seed in five misses no side quest in its first hour, because the heroes learn every microskill before meeting a quest they cannot do.
  • The tally counts overkill in damage and damage taken (its tooltips say so), but not overhealing.
  • Side-quest gold is not raised by the Fat Purse upgrade.

Known rough edges

  • Reviews. Six of this round's pull requests were read by an independent reviewer after they landed (#36, #38, #39, #42, #45, #46). Every one of those reviews found real bugs, all since fixed (#41, #44, #47, #48, #49). The others were checked by CI and the lead only, so expect bugs of the same kind in them: mostly at 4x speed, after a hitch, or on returning to the game mid-fight.
  • The fixes themselves (#41, #44, #47, #48, #49) have not been reviewed.
  • All art is a first pass and stays that way by decision: Zak will make the characters, and the world's look is to be agreed with him. Weakest: the ghoul, clay hound, fire imp and the Clay Colossus.
  • The game still accepts any target depth. The ladder warns before and after a dangerous click, but its rule (the lowest hero's level plus one) is a rough guide, not tested against real fights.
  • The Bone Lord and Deep Maw (depths 15 and 20) were balanced by hand, with no test holding them.
  • The full check takes about eight minutes, most of it the tests; longer when several lanes run at once.

1. Run the game

From the colony folder, in any terminal:

godot --path .

To open the project in the Godot editor instead (press F5 there to play):

godot -e --path .

What you will see today: the party of four walking the labyrinth on its own, seen from the side like an ant farm, with monsters waiting in the chambers ahead. When the party walks into a chamber with monsters, the fight plays out: heroes and monsters wind up and strike, the mage and the cleric throw small bolts (amber to hurt, green to heal), the struck flash white and the dead collapse. Along the bottom, a frame per hero shows its portrait, level, health and XP; top left, the depth, what the party is doing and the target-depth controls; top right, the gold. The game keeps running while the window is minimised.

Fights, coins, level ups, wipes and the other beats of the loop have short 8-bit sound effects, quiet by default. Press M to mute or unmute them; the game remembers the choice (in a small settings file next to the save, not in the save).

If the game fails to start after pulling new work or switching branch, rebuild Godot's import cache and try again:

python tools/dev.py import

2. What the game is

Colony is a 2D pixel-art idle RPG you leave running while you do other things. A party of heroes lives in an endless labyrinth. They explore, fight, loot and rest on their own. You are the manager: you recruit, gear and spec the party, point it at a depth, and check in now and then.

Design pillars

  • Built to be left running. Leaving it alone is how it is played. Progress never goes backwards.
  • The skill is in building the party, not in fast reactions.
  • Flavour over power. Rare things give stories, not a must-have meta.
  • A rich, lively little world. Simple sprites, a lot of character in items and effects.

The full design is in docs/design/plan.md.

3. How a session plays Planned

  1. The party moves through the labyrinth on its own and fights what it meets.
  2. Kills give XP, gold and item drops.
  3. When resources run low the party rests, then carries on.
  4. You check in: spend XP on talents, buy and equip gear, change the party, set a target depth.
  5. You tab away again.

Built: spec 005 The loop runs in the simulation with nobody touching it, and Built: spec 008 the main game draws it (see "The world on screen" below). A party of four (knight, rogue, mage, cleric) starts in the middle of the start chamber and walks at 3 tiles a second, one behind the other, knight first. It has a target depth: above it, the party walks to the shaft and climbs down; below it, it climbs back up; at it, it walks from one end of the level to the other and back, farming. Every chamber except the start and the two chambers with a shaft holds 2 to 5 monsters (more in a den); deeper kinds are more likely once they appear. Walking into a chamber with monsters starts a fight. Each kill gives every living hero the monster's XP and the party some gold (double in a vault); enough XP raises a hero's level (20 XP for level 2, 90 for level 3, 380 for level 5). After a fight, if anyone is dead or under 60% health (80% or 40% under the Cautious or Bold pace, spec 035), the party rests where it stands until everyone is full again (4% of health a second). A cleared chamber fills up again quickly near the surface (5 seconds on depth 1) and more slowly deeper down, 90 seconds from depth 10 on. Each target depth that holds for the stable time is followed by the next one down (auto-push, which can be turned off), so the party keeps going deeper until it meets something it cannot beat.

Built: spec 036 A lively start. The first half hour moves quickly: in a new game the party fights five or more times in its first two minutes, every hero reaches level 2 within half a minute or so and level 5 within about eight minutes, it goes down to depth 2 after about a minute and a half and reaches depth 5 in about eight minutes, and the first camp upgrade is affordable after about a minute. The Slime King on depth 5 usually beats the party a few times before the party beats it. From depth 10 down nothing changed: the deep game is as slow and idle as before.

Built: spec 020 Guardians. Every fifth level (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, ...) has a guardian in the chamber with the shaft down: one huge monster, alone, that the party must beat to go deeper. The Slime King holds depth 5, the Clay Colossus depth 10, the Bone Lord depth 15 and the Deep Maw depth 20 and every fifth level below. A guardian is a wall: four level-5 heroes almost never beat the Slime King, level-9 heroes almost always do (levels 10 and 15 against the Clay Colossus). The party throws itself at it, wipes, falls back to farm, grows and comes back. Beating one pays at least ten ordinary kills' worth of XP and gold, and its chamber then stays empty for 30 minutes so the party can pass freely. Guardians are never met in ordinary fights.

Built: spec 049 Areas. The labyrinth is four areas, not one endless shaft: Mossy Hollow (slimes, cave bats, goblins and spiders; the Slime King), The Claypits (mud golems, clay hounds, ogres; the Clay Colossus), The Old Crypt (skeletons, ghouls, wraiths; the Bone Lord) and Ember Deep (fire imps, crystal beetles, deep horrors; the Deep Maw). Inside an area the party goes down through numbered stages exactly as it went down through depths; the screen names a place as an area and a stage (Mossy Hollow 3). Each area has a base level (1, 6, 11, 16): stage 1 of the Claypits is as hard as old depth 6, but the time to make a stage safe goes by the stage number, so stage 1 of any area is safe in a minute. Each area's guardian waits on its stage 5 and every fifth stage after; beating it the first time opens the next area (The Claypits is open in the journal). A new game has only Mossy Hollow open. Sending the party to another open area takes ten seconds on the road (On the way to The Claypits 1), which rests it as ten seconds of resting would (the fallen stand up), and every area keeps its own safe and furthest stage, so it can go back. The area map (spec 053, below) is where you choose; dev.py sim --area does it for a headless run. An old save opens the areas its deepest depth had already reached past. The labyrinth of each area is its own, and a monster appears in only its own area.

Built: spec 052 Resources. Each area gives its own resource: Mossy Hollow glowcaps, the Claypits clay, the Old Crypt old bones and Ember Deep embers. A monster killed there may drop some: a small one about three times in ten, a large one eight times in ten, a guardian always and several (6 on stage 1). Further in pays more: a quarter more for every stage past the first (a small drop is 1 on stage 1, 3 on stage 9). A side quest finished pays a large monster's drop. A drop shows as gold does: its small icons rise from the monster and fly to the top right, where each resource the party holds has an icon and a count beside the gold (rest the mouse on one for where it comes from); the journal notes the first of each, and the "While you were away" panel lists what was gathered (Gathered 40 glowcaps, 12 clay). Each camp upgrade takes one resource from its fourth rank on: Field Medicine and Quick March glowcaps, Stout Armor clay, Sharp Blades old bones and Fat Purse embers. The first three ranks cost gold alone, so the opening is as it was. In the headless runs a party that stays in Mossy Hollow stops at rank 3 of Stout Armor, Sharp Blades and Fat Purse however much gold it has, and one sent to the Claypits holds clay within a minute of arriving. Resources are saved; an older save loads with none and keeps its upgrade ranks.

Built: spec 024 Guardian fights feel like boss fights. When the party meets a guardian, its name appears large in the upper middle of the screen (SLIME KING, with GUARDIAN OF DEPTH 5 under it) for two seconds, the screen shakes once and a low horn sounds. For the whole fight a wide red health bar with its name sits at the top centre, between the depth panel and the gold; it drains smoothly, flashes white on each hit and goes away when the fight ends. When a guardian dies the screen shakes hard, everything stands still for a fifth of a second, it bursts in three waves of its own colours, a fountain of at least 20 coins pours out, GUARDIAN SLAIN shows in gold and a rising fanfare plays. Losing to one shows the usual WIPED and the defeat note (below), which says whether the heroes were below its level and counts the defeats in a row.

Built: spec 025 Side quests and microskills. Not every chamber is a fight. On each visit, about one plain chamber in three holds a side quest instead of monsters: a locked chest, a stuck door, a hurt wanderer, a rune stone or a tripwire. Each needs a microskill, a small talent a hero picks up as they level: the rogue becomes a Lockpicker at level 2 and a Trapspotter at 6, the knight a Strongarm at 3, the cleric a Mender at 3 and the mage a Runereader at 4. If someone in the party has it, the party stops for 3 to 6 seconds (the HUD says Working: Locked Chest) and gets the reward: gold worth one to three kills of that depth, or, from a rune stone, XP worth two kills for every living hero. Then the chamber is cleared and fills again after the usual time for its depth. If nobody can, the party walks past and the quest waits for a later pass, once someone has learned the skill; a tripwire instead springs and every hero loses a fifth of their health (never down to 0, so it cannot wipe the party). Early on the party walks past a lot; a stronger party unlocks more.

Built: spec 028 Seeing side quests. A chamber holding a side quest shows its prop on the floor near its middle, as far off as monsters show: a chest, a door, a small figure sitting down, a standing stone with glowing runes, or a wire between two pegs (plain placeholder drawings). When the party works one, the hero with the microskill steps up to the prop and works at it with their attack, a soft click on every swing, while the others wait; the microskill's name (Lockpicker) and a small filling bar show over them. Done, the prop bursts into bits of its own colours and words say what happened in the reward's colour (gold yellow, XP cyan): Chest opened, Door forced, Wanderer mended, Runes read, Trap disarmed, then the usual coins or XP. A quest nobody can do shows a grey Needs Lockpicker rising from it, once per visit; a tripwire nobody spots shows a red Sprung!, shakes the screen and makes every hero flash and yelp. A hero who learns a microskill gets Learned Lockpicker over their head with the level-up sound. The journal writes each of these (Chest opened: 34 gold, Runes read: 12 XP each, Passed a Stuck Door: needs Strongarm, Sprang a Tripwire: needs Trapspotter, Rogue learned Lockpicker), and each hero sheet's Skills tab lists the class's microskills: the ones the hero has in white with what they do, the ones to come in grey with their level (Trapspotter, level 6).

Built: spec 038 Rough edges on screen. A side quest out in the dark, beyond the party's light, now shows a small white glint at the prop's top corner that pulses slowly (small, big, small, then a pause, every two seconds) above the darkness, so you can spot it from afar. The glint goes when the party's light reaches the prop, comes back once the party has passed a quest nobody can do, and is gone for good once the quest is done. In a guardian fight, the knight and the rogue no longer stand inside the guardian's body: since spec 048 they stand a sword's reach from it, with a small gap of air between them at rest, so that neither the guardian's lunge nor their own swings ever draw one over the other. The others keep their room behind them.

Death and the last stable depth

There is no permadeath. The game remembers the deepest area where the party survived for a set time (one minute on depth 1, longer each level down, ten minutes from depth 10 on). If the party wipes, it revives there and keeps farming. A wipe costs efficiency, never progress.

Built: spec 005, spec 036 A depth becomes the last stable depth once the party has held it without a wipe for long enough: one minute on depth 1, then a little longer on each level down (about 1:17 on depth 2, 2:47 on depth 5, 7:45 on depth 9), and ten minutes on depth 10 and every depth below. After a wipe the party stands up again five seconds later, at full health, where the shaft lands on its last stable depth (or in the start chamber), and that becomes its target. XP, gold and levels are never lost. A fight still going after 90 seconds counts as a wipe: the party is overwhelmed. If you set a target depth while the party is down, it keeps your choice when it stands up. To watch an hour of this as numbers, run the sim command in the table below.

Party

One to four heroes from a small classic set of classes: tank, healer, melee damage, ranged damage. Balance matters: a party with no healer should struggle at depth. Loot is randomised from standard base types.

The labyrinth

Endless and generated as the party explores, from a seed, so the same seed always gives the same labyrinth. Difficulty rises with depth.

Built: spec 003 The simulation generates the levels. Each depth is a band of earth 120 tiles wide and 16 tall with four to six chambers on one flat floor, joined by low tunnels. Each level has one shaft down, on the right on odd depths and on the left on even ones, so the party crosses most of a level to go deeper. Chambers are plain, a den or (at most one per level) a vault; the first chamber of depth 1 is the start. A level is only made the first time something asks for it.

Built: spec 006 The labyrinth is drawn as a cross-section, like an ant farm: earth with the chambers, tunnels and shafts cut out of it, a ladder down every shaft, and sky and grass above depth 1. Floors have a bright top edge, ceilings and walls a ragged outline. The earth changes every five levels (brown loam, then clay, then grey stone, then deep teal rock, then round again) so going deeper looks like going somewhere. Only the levels next to the action are kept in memory. A camera follows the action smoothly and can shake. A demo shows it on its own (see the table below).

The world on screen Built: spec 008

The main game joins the simulation, the labyrinth and the sprites. The camera follows the party, looking a little ahead of it, and glides down shafts with it. Each hero walks smoothly between the simulation's steps; when the heroes line up for a fight, regroup after one or step onto a ladder, they run to their places rather than jumping. Monsters stand idle in every chamber that holds them on the levels near the party, facing the party's side, and reappear when a chamber refills. In a fight every attack, hit, death and bolt comes from the simulation, so what you see is what happened. A hero who falls topples onto their back and lies there until the fight is won (spec 038). After a wipe the camera stays on the fallen party for about a second and a half, then glides to where the party will revive and arrives before the revival, so you see the heroes, still lying, sit up and stand under the light. If that place is more than three screens away the camera stays on the fallen and cuts there just before they stand. At 2x and 4x the same story plays faster, and it picks up wherever the wipe has got to if you come back to the game while the party is down or the game freezes for a moment (spec 048). If the game falls behind (a long freeze, say), it catches up at once to the simulation's true state instead of replaying old animations.

Built: spec 054 Zones, not depths. The screen shows one stage at a time. The way on and the way back are stone doorways in the back wall (no more shafts or ladders): the party walks into the doorway and is gone, the screen dims, a title names where it is going ("Mossy Hollow 4"), and it opens on the party stepping out of a doorway on the next stage. Built: spec 012 The labyrinth is lit and alive. Underground is dark; each chamber has torches on its back wall that throw warm, flickering pools of light in a few hard-edged rings (pixel art, not a smooth blur), and the tunnels between are darker. Each kind of chamber looks like itself: the start has a campfire and bedrolls, a den has webs, a hanging spider, bones and a nest, a vault has a chest, coins and a gleam of gold, and plain chambers have a few stalactites, mushrooms, a broken crate or a puddle. The mood follows the earth: warm in the loam and clay, cold and darker in the stone, an eerie teal in the deep, where torches burn teal and glowing mushrooms and crystals grow. Nothing here touches the rules, and the same seed always looks the same. It is not in the main game yet: a demo tours it (see the table below).

Combat effects Built: spec 010

Every hit is felt. A number pops out of whoever was hit and arcs up: white on monsters, red on heroes, and a big gold number with a ! for a critical hit. Sparks fly away from the attacker and fall. A crit also shakes the screen a little and holds every character still for a split second; a hero dying shakes it harder. Heals show a green +N with sparkles. A monster that dies bursts into bits of its own colours with a pale puff, its coins spill out, bounce on the floor, rest a moment and fly to the purse at the top right (one coin per 5 gold, up to 12), and blue XP motes fly to each hero who earned them. A level up throws gold sparks and shows LEVEL UP. Bolts leave a short trail and flash where they land. Resting heroes give off a small z now and then; a wipe shows WIPED until the party stands up again in a column of light; a new last stable stage shows MOSSY HOLLOW n STABLE (spec 049). Effects never fade: they shrink, blink or fly away. After a long freeze old events start no effects (their gold is counted at once), and there are never more than 40 numbers and 300 particles on screen.

Abilities on screen Built: spec 056

What the talent trees' abilities do is now seen. When a fighter uses an ability its name rises over its head in its colour (never more than two at once over one fighter). An action's wind-up lights the fighter yellow for exactly as long as it takes; a heavy blow throws a bigger white flash and amber shards; damage to many draws a ring out from the fighter or the target; a heal shows the green numbers on everyone healed; a push slides the pushed back with a little dust; a revived hero stands up where it fell, in the fight. A blow that misses says Miss, and one a shield soaks up whole says Blocked with no number. Statuses show for exactly as long as they last: stars circling a stunned head (and it stands still), a red ! over a taunter, an outline round the shielded that dims as the shield is used, an orange, red or pale blue wash with little motes for burning, bleeding and chilled (their damage numbers take the colour), green motes for mending, arrows up and down for hasted and slowed, a bright or a broken sword for mighty and weakened, a target for marked, and a half-faded sprite for nimble and untouchable. Each hero frame shows up to three small status icons, and resting the mouse on the frame names every status with the seconds it has left. In the party tally, click a hero's line to see its damage and healing by ability (Whirlwind 412, Strikes 950, Bleeding 88); click again to close it. These are the only effects that fade: the half-faded sprite and nothing else.

4. How the code works

The code is in two halves that are kept strictly apart.

HalfFolderJob
Simulation sim/ Every rule of the game: movement, combat, loot, rest, depth. Plain code with no graphics, no clock and no global randomness, so it can run with no window at all.
Game game/ Shows the simulation (sprites, effects, UI) and passes the player's choices to it. Decides no rules.

This split is what makes the rest possible:

Time Built

The simulation moves in fixed ticks, ten per second of game time, and never asks what time it is. A driver measures real elapsed time and steps the simulation that many ticks. Because ticks follow the clock and not the frame rate, the game keeps exact time when unfocused or minimised, and time missed while the game was closed is simply more ticks to work through.

At 2x or 4x (spec 032) the driver steps two or four times as many ticks a second. The extra ticks are a best effort: if a frame has no time left for them, they are dropped rather than saved up for later, so a slow moment never ends in a burst. Time away, saving and running while minimised always count real time at 1x.

Randomness Built

Every dice roll comes from a seeded stream (SimRng). The same seed gives the same run, which makes bugs repeatable and balance testable. Each system takes its own stream, so adding a loot roll never changes what combat rolls. Watching the game never changes it: a game on screen and the same seed run headless (the sim command) play out exactly alike, monster for monster (spec 023).

Content as data Built

Classes, talents, items and monsters are data files under data/, not code. Adding a monster means adding a file. Every data file is checked automatically. So far there are four hero classes (data/heroes/: knight, rogue, mage, cleric) and thirteen monsters plus four guardians (data/monsters/), five microskills (data/microskills/) and five side quests (data/sidequests/). Each band of five levels has its own inhabitants (Built: spec 013): slime and cave bat from depth 1, skeleton from 2, spider from 3, goblin from 4 and ogre from 5 in the loam; mud golem from 6, fire imp from 7 and clay hound from 8 in the clay; ghoul from 11, crystal beetle from 12 and wraith from 13 in the stone; and the deep horror from 16. Deeper kinds are met more often once they appear, so each band is mostly its own monsters, and they are worth more XP and gold. A party of four level-12 heroes usually, but not always, beats three stone-band monsters; a level-6 party almost never does. The talent trees are data too (data/talents/, spec 051); items come later.

Camp upgrades Built: spec 016

Gold buys permanent upgrades for the whole party, rank by rank, each rank dearer than the last (the price of the next rank is its first price times a growth factor for every rank already bought). There are five, as data files in data/upgrades/: Sharp Blades (+5% damage and healing per rank, up to 20), Stout Armor (+5% maximum health, up to 20; buying it also heals by the health gained), Field Medicine (+10% healing speed while resting, up to 10), Quick March (+4% travel speed, up to 10) and Fat Purse (+5% gold from kills, up to 20). Ranks add up: rank 3 of Sharp Blades is +15%. Rest, travel and gold bonuses apply at once; damage and health by the next fight. Upgrades are saved. You buy them in the upgrade shop on the HUD (spec 017). In a one-hour headless run, a party that keeps buying the cheapest upgrade it can afford holds a few depths deeper than one that buys nothing.

Talent trees and paragon Built: specs 051 and 055

Camp upgrades lift every hero at once; talents make one hero different. Each class has a small tree (data/talents/) of eleven abilities (data/abilities/, the rules are spec 050's): a trunk of three, then two named paths of four. A hero earns one talent point at every even level (2, 4 ... 14), seven in all, which fills the trunk and one path. Points are spent in order: the trunk first, then the first node of either path, then that path in order. Taking a node of one path closes the other, so two knights can be different heroes. A hero fights with its nodes' abilities from its next fight on. Ordinary fights last a few seconds, so every action is ready as a fight starts and waits only for its condition (Prayer for an ally below 80%, say) and, once used, its cooldown; a hero tries its newest node first, so a deeper one is not crowded out by the trunk. In half an hour of headless play every action a hero has held for ten minutes is used at least five times and every passive fires, except the rescues (Last Stand, Vanish, Guardian Angel, Miracle, Sanctuary, Rampage), which wait for a fight that goes badly.

  • Knight: Shield Bash, Taunt, Bottle-top Block; then Guardian (Hold the Line, Last Stand, Bulwark, Rallying Cry: shields and taunts that keep the party up) or Berserker (Whirlwind, Bloodthirst, Reckless Swing, Rampage: hits harder the more it fights).
  • Rogue: Backstab, Smoke Step, Finisher; then Assassin (Poisoned Blades, Mark for Death, Ambush, Killing Spree) or Trickster (Fan of Knives, Caltrops, Dizzying Feint, Vanish).
  • Mage: Fireball, Frost Nova, Chain Spark; then Pyromancer (Ignite, Meteor, Wildfire, Inferno) or Frostbinder (Ice Lance, Frost Armor, Deep Freeze, Blizzard).
  • Cleric: Prayer, Blessing, Smite; then Lifebinder (Renewal, Sanctuary, Guardian Angel, Miracle) or Sunpriest (Holy Fire, Battle Hymn, Judgement, Wrath).

Once a hero has earned its seven talent points, every level after gives a paragon point instead, the only place plain strength is bought: Power (+3% damage and healing), Toughness (+3% maximum health) or Quickness (+3% attack speed), with no limit. They are spent on the hero sheet's Talents tab. A fresh start gives a hero back every talent point and opens both paths, for 100 gold per level, outside a fight; paragon points stay. Auto-buy never spends a point. Gold training (spec 029) is gone: a save from before gives every hero back the gold its training cost and all the talent and paragon points its level earned, unspent. On screen the tree is the hero sheet's Talents tab (spec 055); python tools/dev.py sim --spend-talents spends them headless (trunk, then the first path, paragon into Power): on seeds 1 to 5 it beats the Slime King in 9 to 15 minutes instead of 14 to 28, with 0 or 1 wipes before instead of 1 to 4. With auto-buy on as well it wins in 9 to 13 minutes, at the first meeting on four seeds of five.

Fights Built

A fight happens on a chamber floor, as a line. Heroes come in from one side in formation (knight in front, then rogue, mage, cleric, a tile apart); monsters spread over the far half. Every tick, each fighter picks the nearest enemy (the cleric instead picks whoever is most hurt, if anyone is below 80% health), walks into range and attacks on a rhythm: a short wind-up, the strike, then a cooldown. Fighters on one side keep a little distance from each other (knight nearest the enemy, then rogue, mage, cleric), and whoever is held back behind an ally strikes over that ally's shoulder instead of waiting for a turn at the front. Melee strikes land at once; spells, spit and heals fly as projectiles. Damage rolls 85% to 115%, crits double it, armour takes some off, and every hit does at least 1. Monsters get 12% tougher for each level deeper than where they first appear, and heroes grow per level. The fight ends when one side is all dead (or, as a safety net, after five minutes of game time, which counts as a loss). Everything that happens comes out as a list of events for the screen to animate; nothing is drawn yet. The numbers are checked by tests that play hundreds of fights: a fresh party always beats three slimes, needs its cleric against a pack of skeletons, and loses to six ogres.

Abilities in fights Built: spec 050, in the simulation

The rules the skill trees will be made of. An ability is a file under data/abilities. An action has a cooldown and a wind-up and replaces an ordinary strike when it is ready: it aims at the nearest, weakest or furthest enemy, the weakest ally or the fighter itself, can be kept for when an ally or the fighter is low, enough enemies are up, an enemy is right next to it (it never walks for that one) or the target is nearly dead, and the fighter walks into its reach first. A healer with a badly hurt ally heals before it uses an action on an enemy. A passive fires when something happens: the fight starts, the fighter lands a hit or a crit, kills, is hit, falls below some health, loses an ally, or makes its Nth strike; it can have a chance and be once a fight, and can land on the nearest enemy (an ambush). A passive on a strike can make that strike hit harder instead of adding a hit of its own (a reckless swing), and then it never fires on a miss. A passive with nobody to land on (its target already dead) does nothing and says nothing. Either does one or more things: damage (which can ignore armour, hit harder on the wounded, or heal the fighter for part of it), healing, a push, standing a fallen ally back up, or a status. The statuses are stunned, taunting, shielded, burning, bleeding, chilled, mending, hasted, slowed, mighty, weakened, marked, nimble and untouchable (no enemy's blow, spell or poison lands on it, and enemies pick someone else while they can); the same one again starts its time over and keeps the stronger without missing a tick of its damage, and every status ends with the fight. Nothing but a revive lands on the dead, and the dead die only once. Twelve sample abilities (Shield Bash, Taunt, Whirlwind, Last Stand, Backstab, Finisher, Fireball, Frost Nova, Chain spark, Prayer, Guardian Angel, Battle Hymn) are built in the tests. No monster has one, and no hero yet but through a hero's talents: the talent trees (spec 051) hand them out and the hero sheet (spec 055) will show them.

Art Built

Every sprite is typed, not painted: a small text file (.px) where each character is one pixel, using only the 32 colours of the master palette (assets/style/palette.txt). A tool bakes each text sprite into an ordinary image the game loads, and a lint refuses any art that breaks the rules: a colour outside the palette, the wrong size, a gap in the dark outline. Characters come in parts (body, head, arm, weapon) so the game can move them. The rules are in docs/style-bible.md, a first pass for Zak to change. To see every sprite, run the gallery (below).

Built: spec 007 The cast: four heroes (knight, rogue, mage, cleric) and five monsters (slime, cave bat, skeleton, spider, ogre), joined by spec 013's eight (goblin, mud golem, fire imp, clay hound, ghoul, crystal beetle, wraith, deep horror) and spec 020's four guardians (slime king, clay colossus, bone lord, deep maw, drawn 64x64, twice a hero's height), coloured for their band: warm in the clay, cold in the stone, teal in the deep. Each part of a character turns around its own joint (the neck, the shoulder, the hand), and the game moves the parts in code rather than flipping through drawn frames: characters breathe while they stand, bob and lean as they walk, draw back before they strike, flash white and flinch when hit, and collapse when they die. A demo shows the whole cast doing all of it (below); the characters are not in the labyrinth yet.

Text Built: spec 009

All text in the game is drawn in Colony's own small pixel font, typed the same way as the sprites (assets/fonts/pixel.px, one glyph per part). Capitals are 5 pixels tall, letters have their own widths (an i is narrower than an m), and every label uses it without being told to. Coloured text gets a one-pixel dark shadow so it reads over any background. To see every character, run the font specimen (below).

Saving

Built: spec 014 A save keeps the party's progress, not the exact moment: the seed, the time played, the gold, each hero's class, level and XP, the last stable depth, the deepest depth reached, the target depth, whether auto-push is on, the rank of each camp upgrade (older saves load with no upgrades) and each hero's talents and paragon points (spec 051; a save with spec 029's gold training gets the gold back and every point unspent), the resources the party holds and the guardians it has beaten (spec 052; an older save loads with no resources, and counts a guardian beaten when the area it opens is open or the party has been past it). Loading stands the party up fresh at its last stable depth, exactly as it does after a wipe, with everything it has earned; the chambers there are full of monsters again. A damaged or unknown save is refused rather than guessed at. The file is written whole or not at all (to a temporary file, then renamed into place), and the save before it is kept as a backup that is read if the save itself is missing or damaged. The save lives at user://colony.save.

Built The game loads the save when it starts, saves every 30 seconds, and saves when it closes. Tests, screenshots and headless runs never touch the real save. To start over, delete the save file (on Windows: %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Colony\colony.save and colony.save.bak).

Progress while you were away

Built: spec 019 Each save remembers the real time it was written. When the game starts with a save, it works out how long it was closed (at most 8 hours; under a minute counts as nothing, and a clock that went backwards counts as nothing) and plays that time through the same simulation as fast as it can, with the same rules as watching. Meanwhile the screen shows Catching up... and a bar that fills; eight hours takes a few seconds. Then the game starts as usual, with a panel in the middle of the screen saying how long the party was away, the gold it earned, the levels each hero gained, the depth before and after, monsters slain and wipes (and, with auto-buy on, the upgrades bought). A click or any key closes it, or it goes by itself after 12 seconds. No effects or flying coins are replayed for the time away. If you quit while it is catching up, nothing is lost: the save is untouched until the catch-up ends.

Built: spec 031 The panel tells the whole story. "Slain" counts monsters that died (side quests pay gold too, so gold no longer stands in for kills). When there is something to say, it also shows guardians slain with the deepest one's name (Guardians slain 2 (deepest: Clay Colossus)), side quests done, side quests missed and the microskill the misses needed most (Most needed: Trapspotter, a hint for the next upgrade or party), and one line per microskill a hero learned (Rogue learned Lockpicker), up to four and then and N more. With none of these, the panel reads as before.

What the check does

StepFails when
formatCode is not formatted, or breaks a lint rule
boundarySomething in sim/ uses graphics, a clock or global randomness
artA sprite breaks the style rules, or its image is out of date with its text file; or a sound is missing, out of date with its recipe, longer than 1.2 s or too loud
compileAny script has an error or a warning (warnings count as errors)

It ends with check: PASS or names the step that failed. Requirements: Godot 4.7.2 and Python on the PATH, plus uv (or pip install gdtoolkit==4.5.0) for the format step.

7. How work gets done

Colony is built spec-first. A spec says what a piece of the game must do and how we will know it does.

  1. Draft. Zak and the lead Claude session write the spec together.
  2. Approve. Zak approves it. Nothing is built from an unapproved spec.
  3. Build. An implementer agent builds exactly that spec in its own copy of the repo and opens a pull request.
  4. Review. CI runs the check, and a separate reviewer agent compares the spec with the code.
  5. Land. The lead merges it and marks the spec landed.
  6. Playtest. At the end of each milestone Zak plays the game and signs off.

The board and the full process are in specs/README.md.
