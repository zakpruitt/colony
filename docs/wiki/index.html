0. Where we are (17 November 2026): areas and skill trees to check in on

In one line: after Zak played the demo he asked for areas to choose between instead of one descent, and a skill tree of real abilities for every hero. That is now on dev: eight more specs (049 to 056, pull requests #50 to #57), each read by an independent reviewer and fixed before it merged. Nothing is in progress, the full check passes (605 tests), and nothing has gone to main. Every spec after 001 is provisional until Zak has played it. The direction is in docs/design/world.md, "Areas and skill trees".

Play it

From the project folder, on the dev branch:

git pull
python tools/dev.py import
godot --path .

The import is needed once after pulling (new scripts). For a fresh start, first delete colony.save, colony.save.bak and colony.settings in %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Colony. An old save loads too: it starts in Mossy Hollow at the stage its depth was, gold spent on training comes back, and each hero has the talent points its level has earned, unspent.

Fifteen minutes with it

  1. Watch the party change stage. It walks out through a doorway, the screen dims with the next stage's name, and it steps out somewhere new. Nothing goes down.
  2. Spend the first talent point. A hero's second level gives one; a goal says so. Click it, or click the hero's frame and open Talents. Point at any node to read it.
  3. Watch the ability get used. Its name rises over the hero; a stunned monster has stars round its head, a shielded hero an outline, and so on. T opens the tally; click a hero's line there to see damage by ability.
  4. Choose a path. The fourth point is the fork (level 8). The game asks before it closes the other path.
  5. Beat the Slime King on Mossy Hollow 5. With talents and upgrades it usually falls at the first try, about nine minutes in.
  6. Open the map (W, or click the area's name) and send the party to the Claypits. It has its own monsters, its own look and its own resource, clay.
  7. Open the shop (U). Past rank 3 an upgrade costs a resource as well as gold; the row says which and where it comes from.

To see every moment in about ninety seconds, labelled, including the map, the four areas, each class's actions and every status on a row of fighters:

godot --path . res://game/dev/showcase.tscn

When it says it is over, the party is left in Ember Deep with mismatched levels and loses; that is the showcase's leftovers, not the game.

What to judge when you play

  • Does it still feel like going down? The doorway, the crossing and the four looks are meant to end that. The list of stages at the right still runs top to bottom.
  • Is there a reason to go somewhere else? Today the reasons are different monsters and the resource three upgrades need. Is that enough "intentionality", or do areas need more of their own (their own side quests, upgrades only their resource buys)?
  • Are the trees interesting yet simple? Read all eight paths (section 4 lists every ability). Which abilities are dull, which path would you never take? Pyromancer tested weakest.
  • Can you see what an ability did? Names, marks and rings are plain placeholders. Which statuses can you not tell apart?
  • Is it too easy now? With talents and upgrades both used, an hour of automatic play on one seed had no wipe at all, and the Slime King usually falls at the first try.
  • Is the screen too full? The top bar now holds resources too, and a fight has names, numbers, marks and drops over it.
  • Can you compare two heroes? Open two hero windows side by side: can you read a whole tree without touching the mouse? At a 1920x1080 window they cascade instead.
  • The sounds. Still nobody has heard them. Abilities reuse existing sounds.

Decisions waiting for Zak

  • The first guardian is no longer a wall for a player who spends talents and gold (first win at about 9 minutes on four seeds of five; without either, 14 to 28 minutes). Raise the guardians, or leave it?
  • Talent points: one at every even level, seven in all, so a tree is full at level 14. Too fast, too slow?
  • Reset for gold (100 a level). A path is closed while you hold it, not for ever. You said going one way should close the other; is a paid reset within that?
  • Which upgrade needs which resource: Field Medicine and Quick March glowcaps, Stout Armor clay, Sharp Blades old bones, Fat Purse embers. A party that stays in Mossy Hollow runs out of gold before glowcaps (120 to 162 unspent after half an hour), so glowcaps hardly bind.
  • Ember Deep is a wall. A level-19 party without talents wiped 8 to 10 times in 20 minutes there and got no further than stage 3. Not measured again with talents.
  • Abilities changed from the first draft so that they are used in real fights: Bloodthirst answers being hit, not a kill; Bulwark fires when another hero is below 80%; Taunt and Frost Nova wait for two enemies; a hero tries its newest ability first.
  • While a hero has a talent point to spend, that goal takes one of the three goal places ahead of the microskill and upgrade goals.
  • The journey rests the party: ten seconds on the road heal as ten seconds of rest would, and the fallen stand up.
  • Still open from the demo: pace barely changes how often the party wipes; fallen heroes lie on the field in ordinary fights; heroes stand about 10 pixels from a guardian; one seed in five misses no side quest in its first hour; side-quest gold ignores Fat Purse.

Known rough edges

  • Reviews. All eight pull requests of this round were read by an independent reviewer before merging, and every review found real problems that were then fixed. The fixes themselves were checked by tests and the lead, not read a second time. Expect bugs of the usual kind: at 4x speed, after a hitch, or on returning to the game mid-fight.
  • Nobody has watched the new things in motion. The lead looked at stills of the showcase: the map, the tree, the crossing, the four areas, the status row and the top bar read correctly there.
  • A resource icon near a bar may hop a few pixels when it starts flying to the counter.
  • The headless run (dev.py sim) never travels by itself, so numbers for the later areas come from runs started there.
  • Every ability, upgrade, paragon stat and microskill has its own picture now (spec 063). They are placeholders Zak may redraw: see them all with the pictures sheet (section on commands). Can you tell what each is without its name?
  • In the tally, damage by ability is worked out from the order of events; a hero's total is exact, the split between abilities could be off in rare cases.
  • A frame holding two arrivals in different areas (a long return from time away) may draw the events between them in the last area.
  • No monster has an ability yet: only heroes do.
  • The full check takes about nine minutes.

1. Run the game

From the colony folder, in any terminal:

godot --path .

To open the project in the Godot editor instead (press F5 there to play):

godot -e --path .

If the game fails to start after pulling new work or switching branch, rebuild Godot's import cache and try again:

python tools/dev.py import

The game keeps running while the window is minimised or unfocused, saves by itself every 30 seconds and when it closes, and plays out the time it was closed (up to 8 hours) when it next starts. Sound is quiet by default; M mutes it and the game remembers. A new game opens the help window once.

The window. The first time, the game opens maximized, so the UI is drawn at twice the size and is easy to read. That needs 1920 by 1080 of room inside the maximized window. On a screen that has that room only with nothing around it (a 1920 by 1080 screen, where the taskbar and the title bar take some), it opens in full screen instead. On a smaller screen (a laptop) it opens as a 1280 by 720 window, as it used to, and remembers nothing until you change something. F11 switches to full screen (borderless, on the screen the window is on) and back to the window as it was. After the first time the game opens the way you left it: windowed, maximized or full screen, and a window at the size and place you gave it. That is kept in a small file of its own, colony.window, beside the save and the settings file, not in either; delete it to start over. If the remembered place is on a screen that is no longer there, the window opens in the middle of the screen, and a remembered size bigger than the screen is cut down to it. The screenshot tool and the tests never use the file and never maximize.

2. What the game is

Colony is a 2D pixel-art idle RPG you leave running while you do other things. A party of four heroes explores a labyrinth of four areas on its own: it walks, fights, picks up gold and resources, rests and moves on. You are the manager: you choose where it goes, spend its talent points, buy camp upgrades, set its orders, and check in now and then.

Design pillars

  • Built to be left running. Leaving it alone is how it is played. Progress never goes backwards.
  • The skill is in building the party, not in fast reactions.
  • Flavour over power. Rare things give stories, not a must-have meta.
  • A rich, lively little world. Simple sprites, a lot of character in items and effects.

The first design is in docs/design/plan.md; the world and the direction since (the colony of little folk, areas, skill trees) are in docs/design/world.md.

3. How the game plays

The loop

  1. The party (knight, rogue, mage, cleric, in that marching order) walks through a stage of an area, one chamber after another, and fights whatever it walks into.
  2. Each kill gives every living hero XP and the party gold, and sometimes the area's resource.
  3. After a fight, if anyone is down or badly hurt, the party rests where it stands until all are full.
  4. Once it has held a stage long enough without a wipe, that stage is safe and the party heads one stage further (unless you turned Auto off).
  5. Sooner or later it meets something it cannot beat, wipes, and stands up again at its last safe stage. It grows there and tries again.
  6. You check in: spend talent points, buy upgrades, choose an area, change the orders. Then you leave it alone again.

Numbers. The party walks 3 tiles a second. Every chamber but the first and the two with doorways holds 2 to 5 monsters (more in a den; a vault pays double gold). A cleared chamber fills again after 5 seconds on stage 1, more slowly on later stages, 90 seconds from stage 10 on. Level 2 costs 20 XP, level 3 90, level 5 380. Resting heals 4% of health a second. In a new game the party fights five times or more in its first two minutes, every hero is level 2 within half a minute or so, the party reaches stage 2 after about a minute and a half, and the first upgrade is affordable after about a minute.

Areas and stages

The labyrinth is four areas. Inside an area the party moves through numbered stages, each harder than the last; the screen names a place as an area and a stage (Mossy Hollow 3).

  • How hard a stage is: monsters there are the area's first level plus the stage minus one. Mossy Hollow 5 is level 5; the Claypits 1 is level 6.
  • Each area keeps its own progress: its safe stage and the furthest stage reached. The party can go back to an area and pick up where it left off.
  • Travelling: sending the party to another open area takes ten seconds on the road (On the way to The Claypits 1). The journey rests the party as ten seconds of resting would, and the fallen stand up. A side quest being worked is finished first.
  • A monster lives in one area only, and each area's labyrinth is its own. The same seed always gives the same labyrinth.
  • A stage is a band 120 tiles wide with four to six chambers on one flat floor, joined by low tunnels: plain chambers, dens and at most one vault. The way on is a doorway at one end, the way back a doorway at the other, so the party crosses most of a stage to go further.

Safe stages, the target and Auto

  • The target is the stage the party is trying to be on. Short of it, the party heads on; past it, it heads back; on it, it walks from one end to the other and back, hunting.
  • Safe (stable). A stage becomes safe once the party has held it without a wipe for long enough: one minute on stage 1, about 1:17 on stage 2, 2:47 on stage 5, 7:45 on stage 9, and ten minutes from stage 10 on. The time goes by the stage's number, so stage 1 of any area is safe in a minute.
  • Auto (on in a new game) moves the target one stage further each time the party has held its target for the safe time. Turn it off and the party stays where you put it.
  • A wipe costs efficiency, never progress. Five seconds after the last hero falls the party stands up at full health on the area's last safe stage, which becomes its target. XP, gold, levels and resources are never lost. A fight still going after 90 seconds counts as a wipe.

Guardians

Each area's guardian waits alone in the chamber with the doorway on, on stage 5 and every fifth stage after: one huge monster the party must beat to go further. It is meant as a wall: four level-5 heroes without talents almost never beat the Slime King, level-9 heroes almost always do. Beating one pays at least ten ordinary kills' worth of XP and gold and always drops several of the area's resource, and its chamber then stays empty for 30 minutes so the party can pass freely. The first win over an area's guardian opens the next area. Guardians are never met in ordinary fights, and the game remembers which are beaten.

A guardian fight is staged as a boss fight: its name appears large (SLIME KING, with GUARDIAN OF MOSSY HOLLOW 5 under it), the screen shakes and a horn sounds; a wide red health bar sits at the top centre for the whole fight; and when it dies the screen shakes hard, it bursts in three waves, at least 20 coins pour out, GUARDIAN SLAIN shows in gold and a fanfare plays.

Resources

Each area gives its own resource. A monster killed there may drop some: a small one about three times in ten, a large one eight times in ten, a guardian always and several (6 on stage 1). Further in pays more: a quarter more for every stage past the first. A side quest finished pays a large monster's drop. Resources pay for camp upgrades past their third rank (see "Camp upgrades"), so what you want to build decides where the party goes. They are saved, and never lost in a wipe.

Side quests and microskills

Not every chamber is a fight. About three plain chambers in ten hold a side quest instead of monsters. Each needs a microskill, a small knack a hero picks up at a certain level.

If nobody can, the party walks past and the quest waits for a later visit; a tripwire instead springs and every hero loses a fifth of their health (never down to 0, so it cannot wipe the party). A quest out in the dark shows a small white glint so you can spot it from afar.

Camp upgrades

Gold buys permanent upgrades for the whole party, rank by rank, each rank dearer than the last. The first three ranks cost gold alone; from the fourth, each rank also costs the upgrade's resource.

Party orders

Two standing orders the party follows by itself (O):

  • Pace: how hurt it may get before it rests after a fight. Cautious (any hero below 80% health), Steady (below 60%, the usual) or Bold (only below 40%).
  • Guardians: Challenge (the usual) or Hold, where the party turns back at the guardian's door and keeps hunting on that stage until you choose Challenge.

Time away

Each save remembers the real time it was written. When the game starts it works out how long it was closed (at most 8 hours; under a minute counts as nothing) and plays that time through the same rules as fast as it can, behind a Catching up... bar; eight hours takes a few seconds. Then the "While you were away" window opens by itself in the middle of the screen, in front of every other window (the windows you left open come back under it), and says as a short list, the numbers lined up on the right, how long the party was away, the gold it earned, the resources it gathered (Gathered 40 glowcaps, 12 clay), the levels each hero gained, where it was and where it is now, monsters slain, wipes, guardians slain, side quests done and missed (with the microskill the misses needed most), microskills learned and, with auto-buy on, upgrades bought. Its Back to the game button or Esc closes it; left alone it goes after 12 seconds, but never while the mouse is on it.

4. Heroes, talents and paragon

Four heroes: the Knight (tank, in front), the Rogue (striker), the Mage (caster, at range) and the Cleric (healer). Camp upgrades lift all of them at once; talents make one hero different from another.

The rules of a tree

  • Each class has a tree of eleven abilities: a trunk of three, then two named paths of four.
  • A hero earns one talent point at every even level (2, 4 ... 14): seven in all, which fills the trunk and one path.
  • Points are spent in order: the trunk first, then the first node of either path, then that path in order. Taking a node of one path closes the other. The game asks before it does.
  • Every node is an ability: an action the hero does in a fight (with a cooldown), or a passive that fires when something happens. None is a plain bonus to damage or health.
  • A hero uses its abilities from its next fight on. Every action is ready as a fight starts and waits only for its condition (Prayer waits for a hurt ally) and, once used, its cooldown. A hero tries its newest node first.
  • A reset gives a hero back every talent point and opens both paths again, for 100 gold per level, outside a fight. Paragon points stay.

Paragon

Once a hero has earned its seven talent points (level 14), every level after gives a paragon point instead: the only place plain strength is bought. Each point adds 3% to one of three stats, with no limit: Power (damage and healing), Toughness (maximum health) or Quickness (attack speed). They are spent on the Talents tab, on the stat's medallion. Buying training with gold (spec 029) is gone; a save from before gets that gold back.

5. The screen

The labyrinth is seen from the side, like an ant farm, with the party in the middle and the controls at the edges. The world's picture is 640 by 360 pixels, scaled up by a whole number with hard edges.

Two layers. Over the world sits a sharper UI layer, drawn in finer pixels (at least 960 by 540 of them, up to about 1280 by 720), with its own readable font: taller letters, real descenders, numbers all one width, and 1 l I, 0 O, 5 S drawn apart. The frame of the screen is on it: the place panel, the countdown, the purse, the boss bar and the hero frames, with their tooltips (a bold title, then a sentence or two, after the mouse rests 0.4 s). Help, the journal, the orders, the shop, the area map, the party tally, the hero windows and the "While you were away" report are on it too, as windows, and so are the goals, the stage list, the ticker, the note after a defeat and the Catching up... bar. The world's own coarse font is left only for what belongs to the world: names and numbers over the fighters, banners like WIPED, and the titles of a crossing. Windows are drawn over the goals and the stage list, as over the world; the note after a defeat is drawn over every window.

Windows

Help (H), the journal (J), the party's orders (O), the shop (U), the area map (W), the party tally (T) and each hero's window (click its frame) are windows on the UI layer. Each has a title bar with its name and a close button. Drag a window by its title bar to put it anywhere; it can hang off an edge, but its title bar always stays on screen, and if the game's window shrinks a window is moved wholly back on screen. Click anywhere on a window to bring it to the front. Any number can be open at once, and the game keeps running behind them; a click on a window never reaches the world or a window under it, and pointing at a window shows no hint for what is under it. Esc closes only the front window. The game remembers which windows are open and where each one you dragged is, in the settings file beside the sound setting (not in the save); a window you never moved sits in its usual place for the size the game's window has. Windows left open open again as the game starts, under the "While you were away" report, and the last button on the button bar puts them all back where a new game has them.

The tally stands at the top left, the shop at the top right and the map between them, so the three can be open together without covering each other or the hero frames. Any window can stay open beside any other. The keys a window answers to work only while it is the front window (the last one opened or clicked): 1 to 5 and B for the shop, 1 to 3 for a hero window on its Talents tab, Q E for a hero window or the tally. The journal (J) and the ticker

The world on screen

  • One stage at a time. The way on and the way back are stone doorways in the back wall. The party walks into a doorway and is gone, the screen dims, a title names where it is going (Mossy Hollow 4), and it opens on the party stepping out of a doorway on the next stage. The crossing lasts as long as the game takes to change stage: about four seconds at 1x.
  • A journey to another area holds the dark screen for its ten seconds with Leaving Mossy Hollow and then the arrival's name.
  • Each area has its own look: Mossy Hollow brown loam, the Claypits red clay, the Old Crypt grey-blue stone, Ember Deep dark teal, each with its own torchlight, and every stage a little darker than the one before.
  • Lit and alive. Chambers have torches that throw warm, flickering pools of light in hard-edged rings; tunnels are darker; the party carries its own light. The start has a campfire and bedrolls, a den webs and bones, a vault a chest and coins. Embers rise, dust drifts, water drips, beetles and moths wander.
  • The arrow. While the party is heading for another stage a small yellow arrow over its heads points the way it is going.
  • A wipe. The party lies where it fell; if it revives on another stage the screen dims, names the safe stage and opens on the heroes standing up there.
  • If the game falls behind (a long freeze, 4x speed), the screen jumps to the true state instead of replaying old animations.

What a fight looks like

  • Hits: a number pops out of whoever was hit: white on monsters, red on heroes, big and gold with a ! for a critical hit, which also shakes the screen a little. Heals are a green +N.
  • Deaths: a monster bursts into bits of its own colours; its coins bounce and fly to the gold counter, its resource icons to theirs, and blue XP motes to each hero. A fallen hero topples and lies there until the fight is won.
  • Moves: every ability has a move the hero makes with its whole body: the Knight leaps into a Shield Bash and spins a Whirlwind, the Rogue dashes through a Backstab and throws Fan of Knives, the Mage lifts off the floor to cast and raises both arms to channel a Meteor, the Cleric bows its head to pray. A passive that fires is a small hop or flick that does not stop what the hero is doing. The blow still lands on the very tick the fight says. To see every move in a loop: godot --path . res://game/entities/character_demo.tscn.
  • Spells: a fireball flies with a flame trail and bursts, a meteor falls from above, an ice shard flies and shatters, frost spreads along the floor, flakes fall for a blizzard, sparks jump from enemy to enemy, a column of pale gold light stands on whoever is healed, a beam falls for a smite, knives fan out. Fire is orange, frost pale blue, holy pale gold, shadow violet.
  • Abilities: the ability's name rises over the fighter. An action's wind-up lights the fighter yellow; a heavy blow throws a bigger flash; damage to many draws a ring; a push slides the pushed back with a little dust; a revived hero stands up where it fell. A blow that misses says Miss, and one a shield soaks up whole says Blocked.

6. How the code works

The code is in two halves that are kept strictly apart.

HalfFolderJob
Simulation sim/ Every rule of the game: movement, fights, abilities, areas, rest, rewards. Plain code with no graphics, no clock and no global randomness, so it can run with no window at all.
Game game/ Shows the simulation (sprites, effects, HUD) and passes the player's choices to it as commands. Decides no rules.

Time

The simulation moves in fixed ticks, ten per second of game time, and never asks what time it is. A driver measures real elapsed time and steps the simulation that many ticks, so the game keeps exact time when unfocused or minimised, and time missed while the game was closed is simply more ticks to work through. At 2x or 4x the driver steps two or four times as many ticks; if a frame has no time for the extra ones they are dropped, not saved up. Time away and saving always count real time at 1x.

Randomness

Every dice roll comes from a seeded stream (SimRng). The same seed gives the same run, which makes bugs repeatable and balance testable. Each system takes its own stream, so adding a resource roll never changes what combat rolls. Watching the game never changes it: a game on screen and the same seed run headless play out exactly alike.

Events

The simulation reports what happened as a list of events (a hit, a death, a status begun, an area opened). The screen draws from those events, in order, and not from the simulation's state at the end of the frame. That matters when many ticks arrive in one frame (4x speed, a hitch): the most common kind of bug in this project has been a view reading the live state too early.

Content as data

Classes, monsters, areas, abilities, talent trees, upgrades, resources, microskills and side quests are data files under data/, not code. Adding a monster means adding a file. Every data file is checked automatically. Today: 4 hero classes, 13 monsters and 4 guardians, 4 areas, 4 resources, 44 abilities in 4 talent trees, 5 upgrades, 5 microskills and 5 side quests.

Fights

A fight happens on a chamber floor, as a line. Heroes come in from one side in formation; monsters spread over the far half. Every tick, each fighter picks the nearest enemy (the cleric instead picks whoever is most hurt, if anyone is below 80% health), walks into range and attacks on a rhythm: a short wind-up, the strike, then a cooldown. Melee strikes land at once; spells, spit and heals fly as projectiles. Damage rolls 85% to 115%, crits double it, armour takes some off, and every hit does at least 1. Monsters get 12% tougher for each level above the one they first appear at, and heroes grow per level. The fight ends when one side is all dead, or as a loss after 90 seconds.

Abilities

An ability is a file under data/abilities/. An action has a cooldown and a wind-up and replaces an ordinary strike when it is ready: it aims at the nearest, weakest or furthest enemy, the weakest ally or the fighter itself, and can wait for a condition (an ally or the fighter is low, enough enemies are up, an enemy is right next to it, the target is nearly dead). A passive fires when something happens: the fight starts, the fighter lands a hit or a crit, kills, is hit, falls below some health, loses an ally, or makes its Nth strike; it can have a chance and be once a fight. Either does one or more things: damage, healing, a push, standing a fallen ally back up, or a status. The same status again starts its time over and keeps the stronger; every status ends with the fight. No monster has an ability yet.

Art, text and sound

Every sprite is typed, not painted: a small text file (.px) where each character is one pixel, using only the 32 colours of the master palette (assets/style/palette.txt). A tool bakes each into an image, and a lint refuses art that breaks the rules. Characters come in parts (body, head, arm, weapon) that the game moves in code: they breathe, bob as they walk, draw back before they strike, flinch when hit, hop on a kill, cheer a won fight, make each ability's move and collapse when they die. Guardians are drawn 64x64, twice a hero's height. All text uses Colony's own pixel fonts, typed the same way: the small one in the world's pixels, and a taller, more readable one (with a bold) on the UI layer. The 16 sound effects are baked from text recipes (.sfx). The rules are in docs/style-bible.md. All of it is placeholder: Zak makes the characters.

Saving

A save keeps the party's progress, not the exact moment: the seed, the time played, the gold and resources, each hero's class, level, XP, talents and paragon points, the area the party is in, each area's safe and furthest stage, the open areas and beaten guardians, the target, the Auto and auto-buy switches, the orders and each upgrade's rank. Loading stands the party up fresh at its last safe stage, as after a wipe. Older saves are brought forward (the save is at version 8); a damaged or unknown one is refused rather than guessed at. The file is written whole or not at all, and the save before it is kept as a backup. It lives at %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Colony\colony.save; delete it and colony.save.bak to start over. Tests, screenshots and headless runs never touch it.

The technical version of this section is docs/architecture.md.

9. How work gets done

Colony is built spec-first. A spec says what a piece of the game must do and how we will know it does.

  1. Draft. Zak and the lead Claude session write the spec together. A spec the lead writes alone from Zak's direction is marked provisional until Zak has read it or played the result.
  2. Build. An implementer agent builds exactly that spec in its own copy of the repo, tests first, and opens a pull request into dev.
  3. Review. CI runs the check, and a separate reviewer agent, told nothing about how the build went, compares the spec with the code and tries to break the tests.
  4. Land. The lead merges it into dev as one commit and marks the spec landed.
  5. Playtest. Zak plays dev. main moves only when he promotes it.
